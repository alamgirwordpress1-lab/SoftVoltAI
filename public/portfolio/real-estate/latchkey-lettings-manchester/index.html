04bRenters' Rights Act 2025

The new rules, in plain English.

The biggest change to renting in England in a generation. Here's the short version. The detail depends on your property and your tenancy, so we'll walk you through it.

Change 1

Section 21 "no fault" evictions have ended

Landlords now need a legal reason, called a ground, to end a tenancy. Selling, moving back in and serious rent arrears are among them.

That makes good records matter more than ever: inspections, rent history and certificates.

Change 2

Tenancies are periodic

No more fixed terms. Tenancies roll on month by month, and tenants can leave by giving notice.

The job now is keeping good tenants happy and keeping empty weeks short.

Change 3

Rent goes up once a year, at most

Increases follow a formal notice and should be in line with the local market. Tenants can challenge an increase they think is too high.

We'll help you set a rent that's fair and that sticks.

What we're doing about it

  • Rewritten every tenancy agreement and letter we send.
  • An evidence file for every managed home: inspections, rent records and certificates, kept up to date.
  • Rent reviews done properly, once a year, backed by local comparables.
  • Short landlord briefings at the office. Ask and we'll add you to the list.

Not sure what it means for your flat? Book a free 20-minute call and we'll go through your tenancy line by line.

Ask us something else
What does Fully managed actually include?

Everything from finding the tenant to getting the keys back:

  • Marketing, viewings, referencing and Right to Rent checks
  • Rent collection, arrears chasing and monthly statements
  • Our 24/7 repairs line and contractor network, within a spending limit you set
  • Inspections every six months, with photos
  • Gas, electrical, EPC and alarm dates tracked and booked
  • Rent reviews, notices and the check-out and deposit return

It's 15.6% of the rent collected, including VAT.

When will the rent reach my account?

We collect on the due date and pay it out within two working days, with a statement showing the rent, our fee and any repairs. You'll get a year-end summary for your tax return in April.

What can a tenant be charged?

The Tenant Fees Act 2019 sets the limits in England. A holding deposit can be no more than one week's rent. A security deposit can be no more than five weeks' rent where the annual rent is under £50,000, or six weeks' if it's £50,000 or more.

Beyond rent and deposits, only a short list of payments is allowed, such as changes the tenant asks for (capped at £50 unless costs are higher) and replacing lost keys. We never charge tenants admin or referencing fees.

Is my money protected?

Yes. Rent and deposits sit in a separate client account, never in our business account, and we're members of a client money protection scheme and a property redress scheme (placeholder memberships for this template). Certificates are linked in the footer.

What does the Renters' Rights Act mean for my tenancy?

In short: Section 21 "no fault" evictions have ended, tenancies are periodic rather than fixed-term, and rent can go up no more than once a year. The changes are being introduced in stages and the details depend on your tenancy, so we'd rather talk it through than guess. Book a call and we'll go through your paperwork with you.

Can I switch to Latchkey from another agent?

Usually, yes. We check the notice terms in your current agreement, then handle the handover: the deposit transfer, the certificates, the keys and a friendly introduction to your tenants so nothing falls through the gap.

What happens if my tenant stops paying?

We chase from the first day it's late, by phone, text and letter, and talk to the tenant early, because most arrears start with a problem that can be solved. If you take rent guarantee insurance (£25 a month), it covers the rent and legal costs while it's resolved, subject to the policy terms.

Do I need a licence to let my property?

Possibly. Parts of Manchester and Salford run selective licensing schemes, and most shared houses need an HMO licence. We check your postcode at the valuation and, if you need one, help you apply before the tenancy starts.

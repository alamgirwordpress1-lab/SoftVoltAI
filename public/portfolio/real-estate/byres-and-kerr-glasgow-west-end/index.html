Buying in Scotland

Five steps from "we love it" to the keys

The Scottish system moves fast at the start and is binding sooner than in England. Here's the order things happen in, and where a solicitor makes the difference.

  1. 1

    Free, no commitment

    Note interest

    Your solicitor tells the selling agent you're interested. It costs nothing and binds you to nothing, but it means you're told if a closing date is set.

  2. 2

    Before you view twice

    Read the Home Report

    A survey, an energy report and the seller's questionnaire in one pack, free to download. Lenders base their valuation on it.

  3. 3

    In writing, by a solicitor

    Make an offer

    A formal written offer with your price, entry date and conditions. With little interest, a seller may accept it there and then.

  4. 4

    One sealed bid each

    Closing date

    With several notes of interest, the seller sets a date and time, often a weekday at 12 noon. Everyone submits one best offer. There is no second round.

  5. 5

    Binding, then keys

    Missives and entry

    The solicitors swap formal letters until the deal is agreed. Once missives conclude, both sides are bound. On the entry date the money moves and you get the keys.

Typical timeline: 6 to 10 weeks from an accepted offer to the keys. Missives can conclude in days when the seller's agent and solicitor sit in the same office, which is how we work.

Sample report · 1/2, 23 Fernlea Street

Rhones and downpipes

3Category 3Urgent repairs or replacement needed now

What the surveyor wrote

Cast iron rhones and downpipes. Leaking joint to the rear rhone with water staining to the stonework below. Repair is required.

In plain English

Rhones are gutters. One joint is leaking down the back wall, and left alone that water finds its way inside as damp. It's the only urgent item in this report, and it's a small job for a roofer with a cherry picker.

What it means for your offer

Ask whether it has been fixed since the survey. If not, offer as planned but ask for the repair before entry. Some lenders hold back part of the loan until a Category 3 is sorted.

"Offers over" and closing dates, in plain English

"Offers over £245,000" is the lowest figure the seller will look at, not the price. It's set to draw interest, so popular flats usually go above it.

  1. You note interest through a solicitor. Free, and it puts you on the list.
  2. A closing date is set once a few buyers are keen. Everyone gets the same date and time.
  3. One sealed offer each, usually by 12 noon. No second round, so lead with your real number.
  4. The seller chooses, often within the hour. Not always the highest: a clean offer with a sensible entry date can win.

Questions

The things buyers actually ask

What does "offers over" actually mean?

It's the lowest figure the seller has agreed to consider. It isn't a target price. In busy parts of the West End most flats sell above it, while quieter flats can go at or just over the figure.

How far over the Home Report value should we go?

Only as far as you can fund yourself. Lenders work from the lower of your price and the Home Report valuation, so anything above the valuation comes from your savings, not your mortgage. Use the slider in the Home Reports section to see the gap.

Does noting interest commit us to anything?

No. A note of interest is a courtesy that makes sure you're told if a closing date is set. You can note interest in as many homes as you like, and it costs nothing.

Who pays for the Home Report?

The seller. Buyers can download it free. If you're selling with us, we book the surveyor and pass the cost on at cost, from £480 for a flat.

What happens on a closing date?

Every buyer's solicitor submits one written offer by the deadline, usually 12 noon. The seller reviews them with us and usually decides within the hour. There's no second round and the seller doesn't have to accept the highest offer.

When is the deal binding?

When missives are concluded. Until then either side can walk away, although it's rare once an offer is accepted. After conclusion, backing out has real legal and financial consequences.

Can you act for us if we're buying a flat you're selling?

Not for the legal work on that purchase: a solicitor can't normally act for both buyer and seller. We'll recommend three firms who know the area, and we're happy to act for you on anything you're selling.

How long from going live to getting the keys?

Our median is 11 days to a closing date, then 6 to 10 weeks to the date of entry. The entry date is agreed in the missives, so you can plan around a lease ending or a school term.

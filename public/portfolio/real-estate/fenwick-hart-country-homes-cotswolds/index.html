Selling a country home

Shown in its best season, to the right people

A country house is bought on a feeling, usually in the first ten seconds of the brochure. So we take our time over the pictures, fly the land, and print something worth keeping on the kitchen table.

Photography by season

We book the photographer for the month the house looks its best, and re-shoot if a listing runs into another season.

Summer: long evenings and full borders. We shoot gardens at 7pm, when the stone turns gold.

Drone and land film

Our pilot (CAA-registered, placeholder) flies every farm and estate, with lot boundaries, footpaths and acreages drawn over the footage.

  • Stills, a two-minute film and a plan overlay
  • Flown at first light, when hedges throw shadows

Brochures worth keeping

Printed on uncoated stock with a hand-drawn location map, the floor plans and the land plan, then posted to our private buyers before the portals see it.

  • A private register of 1,900 buyers, a third from London
  • Digital edition with the material information

Also in every instruction

  • Viewings led by the negotiator who knows the house, never someone on their first visit.
  • Measured floor plans, plus a land plan drawn from the HM Land Registry title.
  • Rights of way, wayleaves, listing and overage set out before launch, not at exchange.
  • A feedback call every Friday, with numbers: enquiries, viewings, second viewings.

From the first walk round to the keys

    A walk round, house and land

    We walk the rooms, the boundaries and the footpaths with the title plan in hand, then advise on price and whether to sell whole or in lots.

  2. ii

    Photographed in season

    If the wisteria is a fortnight away, we wait. Drone, twilight and interiors are shot on separate days.

Village guide

Is it the right village? Five we know well

Schools, pubs and the real station run, from people who do the school gate and the 7.12 themselves. Prices are what we've seen agreed in the last year.

Bibury

7 miles north-east of Cirencester. Our Cirencester office covers it.

A river village of weavers' cottages and trout-filled water, busy with visitors at weekends and blissfully quiet by six. Houses on the lanes above the river are the ones that rarely come up.

Station
Kemble, approx. 25 min drive. Paddington approx. 1h 12m.
Schools
Church of England primary in the next village; Cirencester's secondaries 15 min away.
Pubs
Two riverside inns and a shop with a post office counter.
Cottages from £650,000. Family houses £1.2m to £2.5m.

Stow-on-the-Wold

800 feet up, where six roads meet. Home to our second office.

A proper working market town with a square, a butcher, antiques shops and a deli. Good for downsizers who want to walk to everything, and for families on the edge of town with a paddock.

Station
Moreton-in-Marsh, approx. 10 min drive. Paddington approx. 1h 30m.
Schools
Primary in the town; secondary 10 minutes away in Bourton-on-the-Water.
Pubs
A dozen pubs and coaching inns around and off the square.
Town houses from £550,000. Edge-of-town houses £1m to £2m.

Chipping Campden

The northern edge of the Cotswolds, where the Cotswold Way begins.

A wool town with one of the finest high streets in England, a market hall from 1627 and arts and crafts workshops still trading. Very strong community, and a waiting list for the allotments.

Station
Moreton-in-Marsh, approx. 15 min drive. Paddington approx. 1h 30m.
Schools
Primary and secondary schools both in the town, walkable from most houses.
Pubs
Several inns on the High Street, plus a good wine bar.
Cottages from £600,000. Houses with land £1.5m to £4m.

Kingham

On the Oxfordshire side, 4 miles from Stow. Our Stow office covers it.

The London leaver's favourite, because the station is in the village. Two gastropubs on the green, a famous farm shop down the road, and houses that rarely reach the open market.

Station
Kingham, in the village. Paddington approx. 1h 20m.
Schools
Village primary; secondary in Chipping Norton, approx. 10 min drive.
Pubs
Two pubs on or near the green, both with rooms.
Rarely under £1m. Family houses £1.5m to £3.5m.

Painswick

The "Queen of the Cotswolds", above the Stroud valleys.

Silver-grey stone rather than honey, steep lanes, a churchyard of clipped yews and a rococo garden. Better value than the north Cotswolds, and Stroud's farmers' market on the doorstep.

Station
Stroud, approx. 10 min drive. Paddington approx. 1h 30m.
Schools
Village primary; grammar and comprehensive schools in Stroud.
Pubs
Two village pubs and a hotel restaurant with views over the valley.
Cottages from £450,000. Village houses £850,000 to £1.8m.

Questions

What buyers and sellers ask us first

Can I buy the house without all the land?

Often, yes. When a farm is offered in lots, as Coln Barrow Farm is, you can bid for the farmhouse lot alone. Where it's offered as a whole, sellers will sometimes split it for the right buyer. Ask us early, before the land has been promised to a neighbour.

How long is the journey to London, really?

From Kemble, the fastest trains reach Paddington in approx. 1h 10m; from Kingham approx. 1h 20m; from Moreton-in-Marsh approx. 1h 30m. Add the drive to the station and the parking. Every listing shows approximate door-to-Paddington times.

What does "guide price" mean?

It's the level we and the seller expect offers around. "Offers in excess of" means the seller won't consider offers below that figure. Lot guide prices work the same way, lot by lot.

Do you sell farms by tender?

When there's strong interest, yes. Informal tender asks for best and final offers by a set date; formal tender means the winning bid is binding on exchange. We'll advise which suits your land and your buyers.

What is overage, and why is it on some land?

Overage, or clawback, gives the seller a share of any future uplift in value if the land gets planning permission, typically 25% to 30% for 25 years. We show it on the listing so it never arrives as a surprise at exchange.

What's included in your fee?

Seasonal photography, drone, measured floor plans, a land plan from the title, the printed brochure, accompanied viewings and sale progression. The fees we quote include VAT. There's no charge if the property doesn't sell.

Will you tell me about rights of way and listing?

Yes. Public footpaths, bridleways, wayleaves for power lines, listed status and conservation areas are all part of the material information on each listing and on our sale plans.

Can I bring my dog to a viewing?

Usually, as long as the owners agree and it stays outside the house. Walking the land with your dog is often the best way to know whether a place is right.

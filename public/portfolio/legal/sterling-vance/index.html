      When to call us

      Some disputes have short deadlines

      If any of these apply, speak to a lawyer straight away. Waiting a week can close off options that would otherwise be open to you.

      Out of hours and weekends 020 7946 0311

      A partner is on call for freezing orders, injunctions and other urgent applications.

      • You've been served with a claim form

        14 days

        You usually have 14 days from service of the particulars of claim to respond, or 28 if you file an acknowledgment of service. If you miss it, judgment can be entered against you.

      • You think money or assets are being moved

        Act now

        A freezing order can stop funds leaving accounts, but it has to be applied for before the other side is warned. Evidence gathered early makes the application.

      • Your company has received a statutory demand

        21 days

        A creditor can present a winding-up petition 21 days after serving a demand. If the debt is genuinely disputed, we can apply to stop the petition before it's advertised.

      • You've been shut out of a company you co-own

        Get advice early

        Removal as a director, withheld dividends or a share issue that dilutes you may support an unfair prejudice petition. What you do next affects your position.

      • An adviser's mistake has cost you money

        Time limits

        Claims against solicitors, accountants, valuers and brokers usually have to be brought within six years, sometimes three years from when you found out, with a 15-year longstop.

      • A key supplier or customer has stopped performing

        Days matter

        How you respond in the first few days, including whether and how to terminate, can decide which side ends up in breach.

      • You've been served with a freezing or search order

        Act now

        There will be a return date, often within a week, and you may have to give information within hours. Don't move or spend assets before taking advice.

      • An arbitration award has gone against you

        28 days

        Challenges to an award under the Arbitration Act 1996 must usually be brought within 28 days. We also enforce awards for clients when the other side won't pay.

      How a dispute runs

      What happens, and roughly when

      Every case is different, but most High Court claims follow these stages. Before each one starts, we'll give you a timetable and a cost estimate in writing.

      1. Stage 1

        First assessment

        1 to 2 weeks

        We read the key documents, check limitation dates and give you a written view on merits, costs and options. Usually a fixed fee.

      2. Stage 2

        Pre-action letters

        1 to 3 months

        Letters of claim and response under the court's pre-action rules. A large share of disputes settle at this point.

      3. Stage 3

        Proceedings issued

        3 to 6 months

        Claim form, particulars, defence and reply, then costs budgets and the first case management conference.

      4. Stage 4

        Disclosure and evidence

        6 to 12 months

        Documents are exchanged, then witness statements and expert reports. Usually the most expensive stage.

      5. Stage 5

        Mediation or trial

        12 to 24 months from issue

        Most cases settle, often at mediation. If not, a trial in the Business and Property Courts, typically 3 to 10 days.

      6. Stage 6

        Enforcement

        As needed

        If the other side doesn't pay, we enforce the judgment or award, including against assets held abroad.

      Arbitrations follow a similar pattern but with a timetable set by the tribunal. Urgent applications, such as freezing orders, can be made at any stage, including before a claim is issued.

      Questions

      Questions we're often asked

      If your question isn't here, call us. The first conversation is free, and we'll tell you honestly if we're not the right firm.

      Send us an enquiry
      What size of dispute do you take on?

      Most of our cases are worth £1m or more. We take smaller claims where there's a point of principle or a business relationship at stake, and we offer fixed-fee debt recovery for unpaid invoices up to £100,000.

      Do you charge for the first meeting?

      The first call, to understand what has happened and check for conflicts, is free. If you'd like written advice after we've read the documents, we'll agree a fixed fee first, usually £2,500 to £7,500 plus VAT.

      Can you act on a "no win, no fee" basis?

      Sometimes. For strong claims we can offer a conditional fee agreement or a damages-based agreement, often alongside after-the-event insurance. We also work with litigation funders on larger claims.

      How quickly can you apply for a freezing order?

      If the evidence is available, usually within 24 to 48 hours. In very urgent cases the court will hear an application out of hours. Call 020 7946 0311 at any time.

      Will my dispute end up in court?

      Probably not. Around nine in ten of our cases settle before trial, many at mediation. We still prepare each case properly for trial, because that's what gets a good settlement.

      We already have solicitors. Can you take over?

      Yes. We often take on cases at a difficult point, such as shortly before trial or after a ruling has gone the wrong way. Your current firm will transfer the file once its fees are dealt with.

      Do you act for individuals?

      Yes: business owners, directors, investors and partners, in disputes connected with their business. We don't handle family, employment or personal injury cases, but we can recommend firms that do.

      What if the other side is based abroad?

      About 40% of our cases involve a party outside the UK. We serve proceedings abroad, enforce judgments and awards overseas, and work with local lawyers we know in Europe, the Gulf and Asia.

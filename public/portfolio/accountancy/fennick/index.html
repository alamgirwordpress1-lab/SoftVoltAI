Sole trader or limited company: what would you keep?

Simplified illustration. An estimate, not advice.

Assumptions in this illustration

  • 2025/26 tax year, Income Tax rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scottish rates differ and aren't modelled.
  • Day rate × days is all your income. No expenses, pension contributions, student loan, other income or VAT.
  • Personal Allowance £12,570, reduced by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000. Basic rate 20% to £50,270, higher rate 40% to £125,140, 45% above.
  • Sole trader: Income Tax plus Class 4 National Insurance at 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above. No Class 2 to pay.
  • Limited company: £12,570 director's salary. Employer NI at 15% on salary above £5,000 (£1,135.50). No employee NI at this salary.
  • We assume the company can't claim the £10,500 Employment Allowance, because a company whose only employee is its director isn't eligible.
  • Corporation Tax at 19% on the first £50,000 of profit and 25% above that. We ignore marginal relief, so the real bill on profits between £50,000 and £250,000 is a little higher than shown.
  • All profit left after Corporation Tax is paid out as dividends in the same year. Dividend allowance £500, then 8.75% basic, 33.75% higher and 39.35% additional rate.
  • The contract is outside IR35. Inside IR35, the limited company figures don't apply. Accountancy fees and company running costs aren't included.

What is IR35, and do I need a contract review?

IR35 (the off-payroll working rules) applies when you'd be treated as an employee of the client if you weren't working through your own company. If a contract is inside IR35, you pay broadly the same tax and National Insurance as an employee.

Public sector clients and medium or large private sector clients decide your status and must give you a Status Determination Statement. With small private sector clients, your company decides. Either way, a review before you sign helps. Ours looks at the contract and how the work happens in practice, such as control, substitution and whether work must be offered and accepted, then gives you a written opinion for £150 + VAT.

Umbrella company or limited company: which is better?

With an umbrella company you're its employee. Tax comes off through PAYE, there's very little admin and no company to run, but you pay the umbrella a margin and you don't get the tax flexibility of a company. It often suits inside-IR35 or short contracts.

With your own limited company you have more control over how and when you pay yourself, but more admin and responsibility as a director. Since recent tax changes it isn't automatically cheaper, so run the calculator above and then ask us to check your figures.

Can you give me an income reference for a mortgage?

Yes. For clients we write an accountant's reference confirming your income, usually within two working days. Lenders also tend to ask for:

  • your SA302 tax calculations and tax year overviews from HMRC, often for two years
  • your latest company accounts if you're a director
  • for contractors, a copy of your current contract, as some lenders work from your day rate

We can only confirm figures we've prepared or checked ourselves.

When do I pay my Self Assessment tax?

Your online return and the balance of last year's tax are due by 31 January, together with your first payment on account for the current year. The second payment on account is due by 31 July. Each payment on account is usually half of your previous year's bill.

Does Making Tax Digital for Income Tax apply to me?

From 6 April 2026 it applies to sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income is over £50,000. Qualifying income is your total turnover from self-employment and property before expenses, not your profit. You'll need to keep digital records and send quarterly updates to HMRC, and we do that for you on the Sole trader plan.

What are my limited company's main deadlines?

Accounts go to Companies House 9 months after your year end. Corporation Tax is payable 9 months and 1 day after your year end, and the CT600 return is due 12 months after it. You also file a confirmation statement every year, and run payroll each time you pay your salary.

How do I switch from my current accountant?

Just tell us. Once you've signed our engagement letter, we contact your previous accountant for your records and get authorised with HMRC. You can switch at any point in the year and most moves take about two weeks.

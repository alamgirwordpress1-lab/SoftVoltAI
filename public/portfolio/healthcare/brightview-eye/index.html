Your treatment journey

What happens, and when

Most people go from first assessment to treatment in two to four weeks. You'll see the same surgeon at your consultation, on the day and at your check-ups.

  1. 160 to 90 minutes

    Free assessment

    An optometrist checks your vision, scans your eyes and measures them in detail.

    • Drops may blur your vision for a few hours
    • Bring someone to drive you home
  2. 2Same day or within 2 weeks

    Consultation

    Your consultant explains what they recommend, the risks and the alternatives, and confirms the price.

    • Plenty of time for questions
    • No pressure to decide on the day
  3. 3About 3 hours in hospital

    Surgery day

    Most procedures take 15 to 20 minutes under local anaesthetic, and you go home the same day.

    • A nurse stays with you throughout
    • Eye drops and a shield to take home
  4. 412 months included

    Aftercare

    Check-ups the next day, after one week and after one month, and a phone line that's answered 24 hours a day.

    • Most people are back at work within a week
    • Ask us before you drive

Coming for an assessment?

  • Bring your glasses, contact lenses (in their case) and a list of your medicines
  • Leave soft contact lenses out for one week before, and hard lenses for three
  • Don't drive home after your assessment

Emergency eye clinic

Seen quickly, seven days a week

For new or worrying eye problems that can't wait for an appointment. Walk in, or call ahead and we'll tell you how busy we are.

If you have sudden loss of vision, call 999 or go to A&E

Go straight to A&E for chemical splashes, serious eye injuries, or if you've hit your head. For urgent advice at any time, call NHS 111.

Open today

Come to the clinic if you have

  • A red, painful or very sore eye
  • New flashes of light or a sudden shower of floaters
  • Something in your eye that won't wash out
  • A change in your vision since recent eye surgery

How it works

A nurse sees you first and decides how quickly you need to see a doctor. Most people are seen within an hour. A self-pay emergency appointment is £150, and insured patients should bring their authorisation number. If you've had surgery with us in the last 12 months, there's no charge.

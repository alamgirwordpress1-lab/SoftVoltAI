Befriending for older people in Edinburgh & the Lothians

A friendly voice can change someone’s whole week.

We match older people who feel lonely with a volunteer friend, for a weekly phone call, a visit at home or a seat at a lunch club. It’s free, and it starts with one conversation.

How we help

Four ways to stay connected

Everyone’s different. Some people love a good natter on the phone, others would rather share a pot of tea at home. We’ll talk it through with you and find what suits.

  • Telephone friends

    A volunteer rings at the same time each week for a proper catch-up. Ideal if getting out and about is hard.

    Weekly call, 30 to 60 minutes

    Ask for a telephone friend

  • Home visits

    A befriender visits you at home for a cup of tea and a chat, or keeps you company on a stroll to the shops or the park.

    Weekly or fortnightly, about an hour

    Ask about home visits

  • Lunch clubs

    A hot two-course lunch, good company and a quiz or a sing-song. Most clubs can arrange door-to-door transport.

    14 clubs, usually £3 to £5

    Find a lunch club

  • Digital skills café

    Patient, friendly help with phones and tablets: video calls with family, online shopping and NHS apps.

    Drop in, Tuesdays and Thursdays

    Book a café session

Refer someone

Know someone who’d enjoy a chat?

You can refer yourself, a relative, a friend or neighbour, or someone you support at work. It costs nothing, and nobody is signed up without saying yes.

What happens next

  1. We phone within three working daysUsually the person being referred, at the time you suggest.
  2. A coordinator comes for a chatAt home or by phone, to hear about their interests and what they’d enjoy.
  3. We introduce a befrienderMatched on shared interests. A coordinator joins the first call or visit.

    Become a befriender

    Give an hour a week. It means more than you’d think.

    Our befrienders are students, retired teachers, nurses on their days off and everyone in between. What they share is time to listen. Many tell us they’ve gained a friend too.

    • About an hour a week

      A phone call from home or a visit near you, at a time that suits you both.

    • Free training

      A friendly welcome session, in person or online, then regular get-togethers and refreshers.

    • PVG check, paid by us

      Every befriender joins the PVG Scheme, run by Disclosure Scotland. We arrange it and cover any cost.

    • Support all the way

      A named coordinator to talk things through, and travel expenses paid for home visits.

    How to get started

    1. Apply onlineAbout ten minutes
    2. Have a chatInformal, by phone or video
    3. Training and PVGUsually four to six weeks
    4. Meet your matchWith a coordinator there

    You’ll need to be 18 or over, live or work in Edinburgh or the Lothians, and be able to keep it up for at least six months. PVG stands for Protecting Vulnerable Groups: it’s Scotland’s membership scheme for people who work or volunteer with vulnerable adults.

      Who can have a befriender?

      Anyone aged 60 or over who lives in Edinburgh, Midlothian, East Lothian or West Lothian and would like more company. You don’t need a diagnosis or a letter from a doctor.

      Does it cost anything?

      No. Befriending is free. Lunch clubs ask for a small contribution towards the meal, usually £3 to £5, but no one is turned away if money is tight.

      How long will it take to be matched?

      We aim to start telephone friendships within four weeks and home visits within eight. If there’s a wait, we’ll keep in touch, and we can often offer a lunch club place in the meantime.

      Can I choose who my befriender is?

      Tell us what matters to you: a man or a woman, someone who speaks Gaelic or Polish, or someone who supports the same football team. We’ll do our best to find a good match.

      What if we don’t get on?

      That’s fine, and it does happen. Just tell your coordinator and we’ll find someone else. There’s no awkwardness on our side.

      Will a befriender help with shopping or personal care?

      Befrienders are there for friendship. They can walk to the shops with you, but they don’t provide personal care, handle money or do housework. If you need that kind of help, we’ll point you to the right local services.

      How do you keep people safe?

      Every volunteer is interviewed, gives two references, joins the PVG Scheme and completes our training before they’re matched. Coordinators check in regularly with both of you, and we follow a clear adult safeguarding policy.

      Can I volunteer if I work full time?

      Yes. Many telephone befrienders call in the evening or at weekends. We’ll match you with someone whose week fits with yours.

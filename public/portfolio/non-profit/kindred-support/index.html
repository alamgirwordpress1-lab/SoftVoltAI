Our listening line

What happens when you call

Picking up the phone can feel like a big step. Here's what to expect, so there are no surprises.

Listening line 0161 496 0310

Every day, 6pm–11pm. Costs the same as a local call.

  1. 1

    A listener picks up

    You'll hear a calm voice say "Hello, Kindred." Our listeners are trained volunteers from across Greater Manchester. You don't have to give your name, and it's fine to take a moment before you speak.

  2. 2

    You talk, they listen

    Talk about whatever's on your mind, big or small. Your listener won't judge, lecture or tell you what to do. They'll help you think things through at your own pace.

  3. 3

    You decide what happens next

    Calls usually last 20 to 40 minutes, and you can end the call whenever you like. If it would help, your listener can tell you about our groups, counselling or other local support.

Good to know

  • We don't record calls, and your number isn't shown to the listener.
  • You can call as often as you need to. Some people call once, others most weeks.
  • What you say stays with us, unless we're worried someone's life is at serious risk. Read about confidentiality.
  • If the line is busy, please try again in a few minutes. It's usually quietest between 9pm and 10pm.

Not ready to talk? You could come to a group and just listen, or text SHOUT to 85258 at any time.

Counselling for 18 to 25-year-olds

Refer yourself for free counselling

You don't need to see your GP first. Tell us a little about yourself and we'll take it from there, at a pace that suits you.

Who it's for

  • You're aged 18 to 25
  • You live, work or study in Greater Manchester
  • You're not having other talking therapy right now

What you'll get

Up to eight weekly sessions of 50 minutes with a qualified, professionally registered counsellor. In person in Manchester, Stockport or Bolton, or by video.

How long it takes

TodayYou send your referral
Within 5 working daysA 20-minute call with us
About 6 weeksYour first session

We update waiting times every Monday. Last updated 16 November 2026.

If you need help sooner

Counselling isn't a crisis service. If you're struggling to keep yourself safe, please don't wait for us: use the urgent contacts. If you're over 25, your GP can talk you through local options.

      Volunteer with us

      Could you be a listener?

      Give one evening a week to listen. You don't need any experience, just patience, warmth and an open mind. We'll give you the training and support for everything else.

      Free, thorough training

      30 hours over eight evenings, covering listening skills, boundaries and how to look after yourself.

      Support after every shift

      A short debrief with a duty supervisor every time, and a monthly group supervision session.

      Safe and checked

      We'll arrange and pay for your DBS check and take two references before you start.

      Shifts that fit

      2½-hour shifts, 6pm–8:30pm or 8:30pm–11pm, from our base near Piccadilly. We ask for a year's commitment.

      Questions

      Things people often ask us

      If you're wondering about something before you get in touch, you're not the only one.

      Can't find your answer?

      Email hello@example.com or call our office on 0161 496 0455, Monday to Friday, 9am–5pm.

      Our office can't offer emotional support. Please call the listening line in the evening, or use the urgent contacts if you need help now.

      Is what I say confidential?

      Yes. What you tell us stays with Kindred. We don't record calls, and we don't share what you say with your GP, family or employer.

      The only exception is if we believe you or someone else is at serious risk of harm. Even then, we'll try to talk it through with you first. Read our confidentiality policy and safeguarding policy.

      Are you an emergency service?

      No. We can't send help to you or trace calls, and our line is only open in the evening. If your life is at risk, call 999 or go to A&E. You can call Samaritans free on 116 123, any time.

      What can't Kindred do?

      We're not a medical service, so we can't diagnose, prescribe or give medical advice. We're not a crisis team, and we don't replace your GP or NHS mental health services. We're here to listen, connect and support you alongside them.

      Do I have to give my name?

      No. You can call the listening line or come to most groups without giving your real name. For counselling we'll need a way to contact you, so we can arrange your sessions.

      How much does it cost to call?

      Calls to 0161 496 0310 cost the same as a local call and are usually included in your phone plan's minutes. If cost is a worry, just say and we'll call you back.

      Can I get help for someone I'm worried about?

      Yes. You can call the line to talk about how you're feeling as a friend, partner or family member, and you're welcome to share our details with them. If you think they're in immediate danger, call 999.

      How do I give feedback or make a complaint?

      We'd really like to hear from you. Email feedback@example.com or ask any volunteer for a feedback form. You can also read our complaints procedure.

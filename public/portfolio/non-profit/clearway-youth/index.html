What we do

Four ways we back young people

Everything we run is free for young people and their families. Most of it happens after school, in places they already know: libraries, community centres and our hubs in Hackney and Stratford.

How mentoring works

From first form to first meeting

We take our time over every match. Here's what happens after you apply, and how we keep everyone safe along the way.

How young people join

Schools, youth workers, social workers and parents can refer a young person. Anyone aged 13 or over can also refer themselves.

Apply to mentor Read the FAQs
  1. 01

    Step 1: Apply

    Fill in a short form and we'll call you for a friendly chat about why you'd like to mentor and what you could bring.

    About 10 minutes
  2. 02

    Step 2: Training and DBS check

    Join a one-day training session on listening, boundaries and safeguarding. We arrange your enhanced DBS check and pay for it, and take up two references.

    One Saturday, then 2–4 weeks for checks
  3. 03

    Step 3: Get matched

    Your coordinator matches you with a young person based on interests, personality and where you both live, and comes along to your first meeting.

    Usually within 6 weeks
  4. 04

    Step 4: Meet fortnightly for a year

    Meet for two hours every fortnight, in public places or at our hubs. Your coordinator checks in every month, and mentor meet-ups let you swap ideas.

    12 months, 2 hours a fortnight

How we share stories

We only share a young person's words with their written consent, and a parent or carer's consent too if they're under 16. We change names, never show their face alongside their story, and take a story down whenever they ask.

Safeguarding

Keeping young people safe comes first

Every mentor and volunteer has an enhanced DBS check, two references and safeguarding training before they meet a young person. Mentors meet young people in public places or at our hubs, never at home, and all contact goes through channels we approve.

Coordinators check in with every match each month, and young people and parents can contact us directly at any time. We review our safeguarding policy every year, and a named trustee leads on safeguarding for our board.

FAQ

Questions people ask us

Can't see your question? Call 020 7946 0418 or email hello@example.com. We reply within two working days.

Who can get a mentor?

Young people aged 11 to 18 who live or go to school in Hackney, Tower Hamlets or Newham. We prioritise those who could benefit most, such as young carers, young people in care and those at risk of exclusion. It's always free.

How is a young person referred?

Schools, youth workers, social workers and parents can use our referral form. Young people aged 13 and over can refer themselves. We always talk to a parent or carer for anyone under 16.

Do I need experience to be a mentor?

No. You need to be 18 or over, reliable and a good listener. We give you a full day of training and ongoing support from a coordinator.

Who pays for the DBS check?

We do. Every mentor needs an enhanced DBS check before they're matched, and we arrange it and pay for it. We also ask for two references.

Where do mentors and young people meet?

In public places such as libraries, cafés, museums and parks, or at our hubs. Mentors never meet young people at home or anywhere private.

What if a match doesn't work out?

It happens, and that's fine. Talk to your coordinator. We'll try to sort things out, and if we need to, we'll end the match kindly and find a better fit.

When is summer school, and how do places work?

Summer school runs for two weeks in August at our Stratford hub, for ages 11 to 15. Places are free and open for booking from Easter. Young people already on our programmes get priority.

How does Gift Aid work?

If you're a UK taxpayer, we can claim an extra 25p from HMRC for every £1 you give, at no cost to you. You must pay at least as much Income Tax or Capital Gains Tax in the tax year as all the charities you support will claim. Just tick the Gift Aid box when you donate.

Can my company get involved?

Yes. Employers host workplace visits, mock interviews and work experience, and some let staff mentor in work time. Email partnerships@example.com.

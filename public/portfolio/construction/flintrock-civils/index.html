Adoptions explained

Section 104 and Section 38, in plain English

On most new developments the sewers end up owned by the water company and the roads by the council. Getting there takes technical approval, a legal agreement, a bond and inspections at every stage.

We build to the adoption standard from day one, book the inspections and keep the records the adopting authority asks for, so the bond comes back when it should.

The agreements and bonds sit between the developer and the adopting authority. Amounts, fees and timescales vary by authority; what's shown here is typical, not guaranteed.

Sewers

Section 104

Water Industry Act 1991

Who approves
The water and sewerage company for the area.
What's adopted
Foul and surface water sewers serving more than one property, and pumping stations.
The bond
A bond or cash deposit, usually a percentage of the cost of the works, held until adoption.
Maintenance
Typically 12 months after the sewers are complete, ending with a final CCTV survey.

Roads

Section 38

Highways Act 1980

Who approves
The local highway authority, usually the council.
What's adopted
Estate roads, footways, verges, street lighting and highway drainage.
The bond
Usually set at the estimated cost of the works and reduced in stages as the road is built.
Maintenance
Typically 12 months after the final surface course, before the road is adopted.

Working on an existing public road? A new junction or widening on the existing highway is normally done under a Section 278 agreement instead. We build those too.

How adoption usually runs

  1. 1

    Technical approval

    The engineer's drawings and calculations are submitted and approved.

  2. 2

    Agreement and bond

    The legal agreement is signed, the bond is put in place and inspection fees are paid.

  3. 3

    Construction

    We build to the approved design, with inspections at the agreed stages.

  4. 4

    Provisional certificate

    The works are accepted as complete and the maintenance period starts.

  5. 5

    Maintenance period

    Usually 12 months. Any defects found are put right by us.

  6. 6

    Adoption

    Final inspection and certificate, then the bond is released.

Health & safety

Safe digging is planned, not hoped for

Buried services, trench collapse and moving plant are the real risks on a groundworks site. Our controls are simple, written down and checked every day.

  • LogoH&S pre-qualification
  • LogoQuality management
  • LogoEnvironmental management
  • LogoOccupational health & safety
  • LogoSupply chain scheme
  • LogoConsiderate site scheme

Accreditation placeholders. Certificates available on request from safety@example.com

  1. CDM 2015 on every job

    Whether we're contractor or principal contractor, there's a construction phase plan, site-specific RAMS and a proper induction before anyone starts work.

  2. Permits to dig and utility searches

    No excavation without a permit. That means current utility records, a scan with a cable avoidance tool and signal generator, and hand-dug trial holes near known services.

  3. Temporary works, designed not improvised

    Trench support, piling mats and deep excavations are designed and signed off through our temporary works coordinator.

  4. People and plant kept apart

    Segregated walkways, trained plant marshals for reversing and lifting, and exclusion zones around slewing machines.

  5. Sensible hours and clean roads

    Standard site hours are 7.30am to 6pm Monday to Friday and 8am to 1pm on Saturday, unless your planning conditions say otherwise. Wheel washing, road sweeping and dust suppression come as standard.

  6. Trained, carded and fit for work

    Everyone on site holds a CSCS card, plant operators hold CPCS or NPORS, toolbox talks run weekly and we carry out random drug and alcohol testing.

FAQ

Questions we're often asked

Can't see yours? Call 0113 496 0420 or email info@example.com.

Which areas do you cover?

We're based in Leeds and work across Yorkshire: West, North and South Yorkshire, York and the East Riding. For larger packages we'll travel further.

Do you price single elements, or only the full package?

Both. Most clients use us for the full groundworks and drainage package, but we regularly price earthworks only, drainage only or S278 works on their own.

Do your prices include VAT?

No. All tender prices are quoted + VAT. For most construction services between VAT-registered businesses in the Construction Industry Scheme, the domestic reverse charge applies, so you account for the VAT rather than paying it to us.

Can you act as principal contractor?

Yes, on groundworks-led and infrastructure projects. We prepare the construction phase plan, run inductions and coordinate other contractors as CDM 2015 requires.

Will you manage S104 and S38 adoption?

We build to the adoption standard, book and attend inspections, and supply CCTV surveys and as-built drawings. The agreements and bonds sit with the developer, but we'll help prepare everything the authority asks for.

What are your site working hours?

Usually 7.30am to 6pm Monday to Friday and 8am to 1pm on Saturday, with no noisy work on Sundays or bank holidays. Where planning conditions set different hours, we follow those.

How do you deal with buried services?

Every dig needs a permit. We check utility records, scan with a cable avoidance tool and signal generator, and hand-dig trial holes near known services before any machine excavation.

How quickly can you start?

For smaller packages, often within two to four weeks of order. For larger sites we agree a start date and resource plan at tender stage.

Do you offer operated plant hire?

Yes, to main contractors around Leeds, subject to availability. Machines come with a CPCS or NPORS-carded operator.

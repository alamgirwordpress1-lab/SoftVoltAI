Planning & permissions

Planning, Building Regulations and party walls, in plain English

Three separate sets of rules can apply to the same extension. Here's what each one covers and who deals with it.

Planning permission

Permission from your council (Merton, Wandsworth or Kingston, for most of our clients) for the size and look of what you build. Many single-storey extensions and lofts don't need a full application, because they fall under "permitted development".

Often doesn't need an application

  • Single-storey rear extensions up to 3 m deep on a semi-detached or terraced house (4 m if detached), or up to 6 m (8 m) through the prior approval scheme
  • Loft conversions adding up to 40 m³ on a terraced house or 50 m³ on a semi or detached house, with no dormer on the roof slope facing the road

Usually needs an application

  • Two-storey extensions, and most side extensions wider than half the house
  • Homes in a conservation area (Wimbledon has several) or covered by an Article 4 direction
  • Flats and maisonettes, which don't have permitted development rights
  • Listed buildings, which also need listed building consent
Timescale
Councils aim to decide within 8 weeks. Prior approval takes up to 42 days.
Who applies
Our architectural partner, in your name.
Our advice
Even for permitted development, get a lawful development certificate. Buyers' solicitors ask for it.

Building Regulations

Minimum standards for structure, fire safety, insulation, drainage, ventilation and electrics. They're separate from planning: almost every extension, loft or basement conversion needs Building Regulations approval, even when it doesn't need planning permission.

How it works

  • Full plans application: drawings and structural calculations are checked before work starts. This is the route we use for extensions, lofts and basements.
  • Inspections: a building control surveyor (from the council or a registered approver) visits at key stages: foundations, drainage, structure, insulation and completion.
  • Completion certificate: issued at the end. Keep it safe, because you'll need it when you sell.
Timescale
Full plans decisions within 5 weeks, or 2 months by agreement.
Who applies
We do, and we book every inspection.
Also covered
Electrical work is certified by our registered electricians.

Party Wall etc. Act 1996

A law for England and Wales that applies when you build on or near a wall or boundary you share with a neighbour. Its purpose is to stop disputes. It doesn't stop you building.

You must serve notice before

  • Work to an existing party wall, such as cutting in steel beams for a loft: 2 months' notice
  • Building a new wall on the boundary line: 1 month's notice
  • Digging foundations within 3 m of a neighbour's building (6 m in some cases): 1 month's notice

What happens next

  • Your neighbour has 14 days to agree. If they don't agree or don't reply, a party wall surveyor prepares an award setting out how the work will be done.
  • A schedule of condition records the state of the neighbour's property before work starts.
Who serves notice
You, or a party wall surveyor acting for you.
What we do
Tell you which notices are needed and when, and build the programme around the award.
Start early
Serve notices once drawings are final, so the award doesn't delay your start.

FAQ

Questions we're asked at most site visits

Straight answers to the questions homeowners ask us most.

Do I need planning permission for a rear extension?

Often not. Many single-storey rear extensions count as permitted development. That doesn't apply in a conservation area, in a flat, or if your house has already been extended. We'll tell you which applies at the site visit, and we recommend a lawful development certificate either way.

Do you take a deposit?

Not a large one. We take a small booking payment when we confirm your start date. After that you pay in stages, each one invoiced once that stage is complete and you've seen it. You never pay for work that hasn't been done.

Is your quote really fixed?

Yes. It's based on the approved drawings and an agreed specification. The price only changes if you change the specification, or if we find something nobody could have known about, such as an old well under the floor. Any change is priced and agreed in writing before we do it.

Can we stay at home during the build?

Usually, yes, for extensions and loft conversions. We can set up a temporary kitchen while yours is out of action. For basements and full refurbishments, many clients move out for part of the build, and we'll tell you which weeks are the hardest to live through.

How long does a loft conversion take?

Typically 8 to 12 weeks on site. Most of the work is done from scaffolding, so the rest of the house carries on as normal until the new staircase goes in, which is usually in the last few weeks.

What are your site working hours?

Monday to Friday 8am to 6pm, and Saturday 8am to 1pm for quieter work. We don't work on Sundays or bank holidays. These hours are in line with local council guidance on building noise.

Who deals with Building Control?

We do. We submit the full plans application with our architectural partner, book every inspection and meet the surveyor on site. At the end, you get the completion certificate.

Do I need a party wall agreement?

If you're working on a shared wall, building on the boundary or digging foundations close to a neighbour's house, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 means you need to serve notice. You or your surveyor serves the notice. We tell you what's needed and when, and plan the build around it.

Which areas do you cover?

Wimbledon, Raynes Park, Merton Park, Southfields, Earlsfield, Tooting, Putney, Morden, New Malden and Kingston. That covers most of SW15 to SW20, plus KT2, KT3 and CR4. If you're just outside these areas, ask us anyway.

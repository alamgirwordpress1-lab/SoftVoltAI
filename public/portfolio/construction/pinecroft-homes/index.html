The process

From first sketch to front door

Six stages, one team. At each step we’ll tell you what happens next, who does it and roughly how long it takes, so there are no surprises.

A wall panel going up on a two-storey build near Dingwall.

12–18 monthsis typical from first meeting to moving in. Most of that is design and approvals; the frame itself goes up in weeks.

  1. Design

    Usually 4–10 weeksYou + our design team

    We sit down with you (or your architect), talk about how you live and look hard at the plot. You’ll see plans and 3D views before anything is fixed, along with a budget you can test against your finance.

  2. Planning permission

    Usually 2–4 monthsWe submit for you

    We prepare the drawings and supporting statements and submit them to The Highland Council or your local planning authority, then deal with the planners’ questions as they come in.

  3. Scottish building warrant

    Usually 2–4 monthsOur technical team

    In Scotland you need an approved building warrant before work starts on site. We produce the construction drawings, structural calculations and energy figures the council’s building standards team asks for, often alongside planning to save time.

  4. Manufacture

    3–5 weeksOur workshop

    Once your warrant is approved, your wall panels, floor and roof cassettes are made in the dry at our workshop near Inverness, insulated, labelled and wrapped for the journey.

  5. Erection

    2–3 weeks on siteOur own crew

    Panels arrive by lorry and are craned onto your foundations. For a typical home our crew has the frame up, roof on and windows in, wind- and watertight, within two to three weeks.

  6. Finish

    3–9 monthsYou, your trades or us

    From here you and your own trades take over, or our turnkey team carries on to the keys. Before you move in, a completion certificate is submitted to building standards, and we help with the paperwork either way.

Energy performance

Warm, quiet and cheap to heat

Two things do most of the work in an energy-efficient home: how well the walls, roof and windows hold heat in, and how little air leaks out. Here’s what ours achieve, in plain English.

U-value
How quickly heat escapes through a square metre of wall, roof or window. Lower is better. An old uninsulated cavity wall is around 1.0; our standard wall is 0.14, roughly seven times better.
Airtightness
How much air leaks through gaps in the building when it’s tested under pressure. Less leakage means no draughts and lower bills. We test every house before handover and give you the certificate.
Ventilation (MVHR)
A well-sealed house needs planned fresh air. Mechanical ventilation with heat recovery swaps stale air for fresh, filtered air and keeps most of the warmth inside.

Membranes keep the frame dry during the build and the warmth in for decades after.

What is the Passivhaus option?

Passivhaus is a building standard with strict limits on heat loss and air leakage. Homes built to it need very little heating, often just a small heater or the warmth from cooking and people.

We design to the standard, use thicker panels and better windows, and can arrange certification through an independent certifier. On a typical turnkey build it adds around 6–10% to the cost; we’ll show you the figures for your house before you decide.

Finance & VAT

Paying for your build

Self-build money works differently from buying a finished house. Here’s how it usually goes, and where we can help.

Self-build finance

Most self-builders use a stage-payment mortgage, which releases money as each stage of the build is finished. Some lenders pay in arrears, after each stage is signed off; others pay in advance. We set our payment stages to line up with how lenders release funds, and we’ll give your lender or valuer the paperwork they ask for.

Typical kit + erection payment stages

  • 10%When you order
  • 30%Before manufacture
  • 40%On delivery to site
  • 20%When weathertight

We’re a builder, not a lender or financial adviser. Talk to a specialist self-build mortgage broker about what’s right for you.

Reclaiming VAT as a self-builder

If you’re building a new home to live in, you may be able to reclaim the VAT you pay on building materials through HMRC’s DIY housebuilders scheme.

  • One claim at the end. You claim once, after the build is complete, using form VAT431NB with your receipts.
  • Watch the deadline. Claims normally have to reach HMRC within three months of completion, so keep invoices together from day one.
  • Our invoices help. We show VAT clearly on kit invoices, and labour on a new home is usually zero-rated, so there’s nothing to reclaim on our erection work.

This is a general summary, not tax advice. Rules change and every project is different, so check the current HMRC guidance or ask an accountant before you count on a refund in your budget.

Plots & site support

Help with the plot, ideally before you buy it

A good house starts with an honest look at the land. We’ll walk the plot with you and tell you what it will take to build there, before you’re committed.

  • Plot check visit

    Access, slope, ground, drainage, services and the view, followed by a written note with the costs we can see coming.

  • Delivery and crane access

    Panels come on articulated lorries and go up by crane. We check bends, bridges and turning space early, especially on single-track roads.

  • Foundations and drainage

    We provide foundation drawings and loadings, and can put you in touch with local groundworkers and engineers for percolation tests and private drainage.

  • Croft land

    If your plot is on croft land you’ll usually need a decrofting direction before you can build. We’ll flag it early and point you to the right people.

  • Water, power and broadband

    Rural connections can take months. We’ll remind you to apply as soon as planning is granted, so the house isn’t finished before the cables arrive.

We build across the Highlands, Moray, Argyll and the islands. Still looking for land? We keep an informal list of plots that clients and agents tell us about.

FAQ

Questions we’re asked a lot

Straight answers to the things most people want to know before their first meeting.

How long does a timber-frame self-build take?

Allow 12 to 18 months from first sketch to moving in. Design, planning and building warrant usually take the longest, often six to nine months together. Once your warrant is approved we make your panels in about four weeks, and the frame is weathertight within two to three weeks on site. Finishing the inside takes three months or more, depending on who does it.

Do I need my own architect?

No. Our in-house team can design your home and prepare the planning and building warrant drawings. If you already have an architect, we’re happy to work from their drawings and take care of the structural design of the frame.

What’s included in a kit?

Usually the insulated external wall panels with breather membrane, internal stud walls, floor and roof cassettes or trusses, ridge and structural beams, fixings, and a full set of construction drawings. Windows, doors and cladding can be added. Every quote lists exactly what’s in and what isn’t.

Is timber frame suitable for Highland weather?

Yes. Timber frame is the most common way new houses are built in Scotland. Our panels are made in the dry, wrapped for delivery and closed in quickly on site, and we design the frame and cladding for the wind exposure of your plot.

Can I get a mortgage and insurance?

Most lenders and insurers know timber frame well. For a self-build you’ll need a stage-payment mortgage, self-build site insurance and a structural warranty. We provide the drawings, certificates and stage sign-offs they ask for.

Do I have to build to Passivhaus?

Not at all. Every Pinecroft home is designed to do better than current Scottish building standards. Passivhaus is an upgrade for people who want the lowest possible heating bills, and we’ll show you the cost difference before you decide.

Do you handle foundations?

On turnkey projects, yes. On kit projects we give you foundation drawings and loadings, and can recommend local groundworkers we’ve worked with for years. We check the foundations are level and square before our crew starts.

Do you build on the islands?

Yes, by arrangement. We’ve delivered to Skye, Lewis and Orkney. Island builds need extra planning for ferries, cranes and crew accommodation, and we price that separately and clearly.

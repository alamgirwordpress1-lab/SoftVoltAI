03bPace converter

Flat pace to fell time

Road pace means little on a fell. This uses Naismith's rule in the form runners use it: every 100 m of climb counts as roughly 800 m of flat running (Scarf's equivalence), then we add a little for rough ground.

Average pace

Flat equivalent
22.4 km

Over four hours, add 10 to 20% for food stops, darkness and tired legs. Steep, rough descents are rarely quicker than flat running until you've practised them.

04Getting started

From first call to first fell, in about a week.

No forms to wade through and no fitness test to pass. We find out what your weeks really look like, then build from there.

  1. 01

    20 minutes · Free

    A call with a coach

    Tell us about your running, your week and the race on your mind. No hard sell: if we're not the right fit, we'll say so and point you somewhere better.

  2. 02

    90 minutes · Latrigg or video

    An assessment run

    We run Latrigg together to see how you move uphill and down. Further away? A video call and your last month of watch data does the job.

  3. 03

    Within 5 days · In the app

    Your plan, on your phone

    Sessions written in time and effort rather than pace, because pace on a fell tells you very little. Synced to Garmin, Coros or Apple Watch.

  4. 04

    Every week · Until race day

    Check in, adjust, repeat

    Tell us how it went. We move things around night shifts, poorly kids, storms and the odd pub night, so the plan bends rather than breaks.

06Safe on the fells

The hills don't care how fit you are. We do.

Most trouble on the fells starts small: a missed turn in the clag, a soaked base layer, a phone on 4%. We teach the habits that stop small things getting big.

  1. We read the mountain forecast, not the town one.

    Every session is planned against the Mountain Weather Information Service and the Met Office mountain forecast. Cloud base, summit wind and wind chill decide the route.

  2. A leader at the front, a sweeper at the back.

    Both qualified, both carrying a group shelter, a first aid kit and a fully charged phone. Nobody gets left on a summit to catch up.

  3. Turn-back times are set before we leave.

    Summits are optional. Getting back to Keswick for tea isn't, and we'd rather turn round at Little Man than finish in the dark.

  4. You leave with the skills, not just the run.

    Every coached athlete gets a navigation session in their first three months, so you're never relying on whoever happens to be in front.

10Questions

Questions we get asked at the Moot Hall.

Q.01I've only ever run on roads. Is fell running for me?

Yes, and most of our athletes started there. Your road fitness carries straight over. What's new is climbing, descending and finding your way. We start you on trails and low fells, build your confidence on descents, and only head for the big tops once you're ready. The Sunday social and the Thursday lakeshore run are the gentle way in.

Q.02How do you keep group runs safe in bad weather?

Every run is led by a qualified Mountain Leader with a sweeper at the back. We plan against the mountain forecast, set turn-back times before we start, and carry a group shelter and first aid kit. If summit winds are forecast above 40mph, or the cloud is down and the group is new, we switch to a lower route. We'll never take a group out in conditions we wouldn't run in ourselves.

Q.03Will a plan fit around work, shifts and family?

That's the whole point. You tell us which days are fixed, which are flexible and how long you've really got. Sessions are written in time and effort rather than distance, so a 45-minute run works whether it's on the fell or round the estate at 5am. Coached athletes can move sessions around and we'll rebalance the week within 24 hours.

Q.04Do I need to live in Keswick?

No. About half our athletes are coached remotely from Manchester, Leeds, Newcastle, Glasgow and further afield. You'll get a plan built for the hills you can actually reach, video calls with your coach and, when you can get up here, a 1:1 Fell Day or a race recce weekend.

Q.05What kit do I need for my first group run?

Trail shoes with decent grip, a waterproof jacket, a hat and gloves, a small snack and some water. For Saturday long runs you'll need full FRA kit: waterproof top and bottoms, hat, gloves, map, compass, whistle and emergency food. We lend compasses and maps while you're getting started, and the kit checker above shows the full list.

Q.06Can you help me with a Bob Graham Round?

Yes. We plan schedules, recce each leg with you, find pacers and organise road support at Threlkeld, Dunmail Raise, Wasdale and Honister. Most of our Bob Graham athletes have 12 to 18 months of coaching behind them before an attempt. Planning starts from £350.

Q.07What happens if I get injured or ill mid-plan?

Tell us straight away. We'll adjust the plan, pause your payments if you're out for more than two weeks, and work with your physio if you have one. Ollie can advise on getting back to running, but he'll always refer you on if you need hands-on treatment.

Q.08How does billing work, and can I cancel?

Plan only and Coached are billed monthly by Direct Debit, with no joining fee and no minimum term. Cancel any time before your next billing date. Fell Days and Navigation Days are paid when you book, with a full refund if we cancel for weather, or a free move to another date.

The classes

Four ways in. One unhurried method.

Every class is 40 to 50 minutes on the reformer, taught to the same principle: slow, controlled work that you can feel in the right places. Start with Foundations and we'll tell you when you're ready to move on. Nobody gets pushed up a level before they want to be.

Start here

50 min · All welcome

Foundations

Learn the reformer properly: springs, straps, footbar and how to breathe through it all. A slower pace with time to set up, and every exercise explained before you try it.

Good for: complete beginners, desk-stiff backs, coming back after injury or time off.

Your first class

Nervous? Good. We planned for that.

Most people walk in worried they'll be the only one who doesn't know what a footbar is. Here's exactly what happens, from booking to the slightly wobbly walk home.

What to bring

  • Grip socks. Your first pair is free, after that they're £9 at the desk.
  • Fitted clothes you can move in. Baggy tops catch on the springs.
  • A water bottle. We have a filtered tap, no plastic bottles.
  • Ten spare minutes before class so nothing feels rushed.

  1. 01 Before you come

    Book a Foundations class and tell us about you

    Pick any Foundations slot on the timetable. We'll email a three-minute health form: back trouble, pregnancy, surgery, the knee that clicks. Your instructor reads it before you arrive, so you won't have to explain yourself in front of the room.

    • 3-minute form
    • Read by a physio

  2. 02 Ten minutes early

    Meet the reformer before the class starts

    Drop your bag in a locker and your instructor will walk you to your reformer. They'll show you how the springs change the load, where your feet go on the footbar and how to get on and off without the carriage sliding away. Ask anything; there are no daft questions here.

    • Springs explained
    • Socks on us

  3. 03 The class itself

    Fifty minutes, twelve people, one instructor who's watching

    We call out spring settings before every exercise and give an easier and harder version as we go. With twelve in the room your instructor can see everyone, so expect a quiet hand on your shoulder rather than a shout across a gym. No mirrors, low lights, a calm playlist.

    • Max 12
    • No mirrors

  4. 04 Afterwards

    A short note, and a plan for your next two classes

    That evening you'll get a note from your instructor with what they noticed and which class to book next. Expect to feel muscles you'd forgotten about the following day, not to be wiped out. Your remaining intro classes are valid for 30 days.

    • Personal follow-up
    • 30 days to use

      FAQs

      Questions we hear every week

      If yours isn't here, ask at the desk, by phone or in the form below. There's no such thing as a silly Pilates question.

      Rather talk it through? Call 0117 496 0742.

      I've never been on a reformer. Will I be out of my depth?

      No. That's exactly what Foundations is for, and around a third of every Foundations class is brand new. Your instructor sets your springs, shows you how the machine moves before you start and gives an easier option for every exercise. If you'd rather learn on your own first, one private session (£65) covers the basics in 55 minutes.

      £22 a class seems a lot. Is it really worth it?

      It's more than a gym class because the ratio is different: one fully trained instructor to a maximum of twelve, on equipment that costs around £5,000 per reformer. Most regulars pay less than £22 though. The 8-pack brings it to £19, and at three classes a week Unlimited works out under £12. Start with the intro offer at £15 a class and decide after that.

      I have back pain. Is reformer Pilates safe for me?

      For most people with everyday lower back pain, yes, and it's one of the reasons Solace exists. Tell us about it on your health form and Hannah, our Chartered physio, will look at anything that needs a second opinion. She may suggest one or two privates first.

      If you've had recent surgery, a disc injury with leg symptoms or anything your GP has flagged, please get their go-ahead before your first class.

      When can I start during pregnancy, and after birth?

      Prenatal Reformer is open from 12 weeks, once you've had your dating scan and your midwife or GP is happy. After birth we usually welcome you back after your six to eight week check, or from around twelve weeks after a caesarean. Freya checks for abdominal separation in your first postnatal class and adjusts from there.

      What should I wear?

      Fitted leggings or shorts and a top that won't ride up when you're upside down. Grip socks are required on the reformers for hygiene and safety, and your first pair is free. Leave jewellery and zips at home as they scratch the upholstery.

      What's your cancellation policy?

      Cancel up to 12 hours before class and the credit goes straight back to your account. Inside 12 hours, or if you don't turn up, the class is used. We always offer your place to the waitlist first, and if someone takes it we'll return your credit anyway.

      Do men come to classes?

      Yes, about a fifth of our regulars. Plenty arrive via a physio or a running injury, and Thursday's Runners' Strength class is roughly half men. Nobody wears matching outfits, promise.

      How do I get there, and where can I park?

      We're a two-minute walk from the heart of Clifton Village, in a mews off Princess Victoria Street. Buses 8 and 9 stop on Whiteladies Road, about 25 minutes from Temple Meads. There's pay and display parking on nearby streets (check the zone signs) and a bike rack in our courtyard.

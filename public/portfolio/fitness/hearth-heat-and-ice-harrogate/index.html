The ritual

What actually happens in a session

Ninety minutes, four simple steps, repeated at your own pace. A host is on the deck the whole time, and nobody will push you to do anything you don't want to.

First visit? Arrive ten minutes early. Your host will walk you round, show you the timers and answer the questions everyone has but nobody likes to ask.

  1. 01

    Arrive and settle

    10 minutesNettle and mint tea

    Change, shower and pick up a robe. First time? Your host takes you through the pools, the timers and the one rule that matters here: listen to your body, not the person next to you.

  2. 02

    Heat

    80 to 90°C8 to 15 minutes

    Sit high for more heat, low for less. Every twenty minutes a host ladles water onto the stones (the Finns call it löyly) and a soft wave of steam rolls through the room. You'll sweat, your heart rate climbs and your shoulders finally drop.

  3. 03

    Ice

    4 to 10°C30 seconds to 3 minutes

    Walk in slowly, breathe out long and keep your head above the water. The first thirty seconds are the hardest part. After that, most people describe a strange, bright sort of calm. Start in the Shallows at 10°C if you're nervous.

  4. 04

    Rest, then repeat

    5 to 10 minutes2 to 4 rounds

    Wrap up by the fire pit and let your body find its own temperature. Then go again. Most people do three rounds and finish on cold, but there's no wrong way to end. Afterwards, warm up slowly with layers and tea rather than a hot shower.

Safety first

Is cold plunging safe for me?

For most healthy adults, yes, when it's done gradually and with someone watching. Cold water triggers a gasp reflex and raises your heart rate and blood pressure for the first minute or so. Heat does the opposite. That contrast is the point, and it's also why some people need a word with their doctor first.

Check with your GP before you book if you:

We'd rather you asked and came next month than skipped the question.

  • are pregnant, or think you might be
  • have a heart condition, chest pain or an irregular heartbeat, or have had a heart attack or stroke
  • have high or low blood pressure, or take medication for it
  • have Raynaud's, epilepsy, diabetes or poor circulation
  • are recovering from surgery, illness or an injury, or have an open wound
  • take medication that makes you drowsy or changes your heart rate

This is general guidance, not medical advice. If you're unsure, ask your GP or pharmacist, and tell your host when you arrive. What you share stays private. We can't admit anyone who has been drinking alcohol that day.

Timers, steps and rails

Clocks at every pool, handrails on all sides, and the 10°C Shallows for gentler starts.

Four rules in the plunge

  1. Get in slowly and breathe out as you go.
  2. Keep your head above the water.
  3. Count ten slow breaths, then decide whether to stay.
  4. Get out straight away if you feel numb or dizzy, or your skin turns white.

Questions

Before you come

The things first-timers ask us most. Anything else, call us on 01632 960418 and a host will pick up.

I've never done this before. What actually happens?

You'll arrive, change and meet your host, who'll walk you round for ten minutes. Then it's up to you: a spell in the sauna, a short plunge, a rest by the fire, and repeat. There's no class to keep up with and nobody timing you. Most first-timers do two or three rounds. Our ritual guide covers it step by step.

How cold is the water, and how long should I stay in?

The Glacier sits at around 4°C, the Beck at 7°C and the Shallows at 10°C. On your first visit, aim for 30 to 90 seconds, and start in the Shallows if you like. Even regulars rarely stay in for more than three minutes. Longer isn't better: slow, steady breathing is what counts.

Is contrast therapy safe for me?

For most healthy adults, yes. If you're pregnant, have a heart condition or high or low blood pressure, or any of the other conditions in our safety guide, please check with your GP before booking. You'll fill in a short health check-in when you book, and you can always skip the plunge and just enjoy the heat.

What do I need to bring?

Swimwear, flip-flops and a water bottle. We provide a robe, two towels, a locker, shampoo, conditioner and body wash. A woolly hat is a nice touch on winter mornings. Leave jewellery at home: metal gets very hot in the sauna.

Are sessions mixed?

Most are, and swimwear is always worn. We run women-only socials on Tuesdays at 7pm and Sundays at 9am, and silent socials (no talking, no phones) on Wednesdays at 8.30pm.

Can I come on my own?

Of course. More than half our guests do. The fire terrace is a friendly place and your host will introduce you to a few regulars if you'd like company. If you'd rather keep to yourself, that's fine too.

Is there parking, and is Hearth accessible?

There are 16 free spaces in the yard, including two accessible bays, and we're a ten-minute walk from Harrogate station. The changing rooms, sauna and the Shallows are step-free, and the Shallows has a pool hoist. Tell us about any access needs when you book and we'll be ready for you.

What if I need to cancel or move my booking?

Move or cancel for free up to 24 hours before your session. Inside 24 hours we can't refund, but we'll happily move you once if there's space. Members can cancel up to two hours before.

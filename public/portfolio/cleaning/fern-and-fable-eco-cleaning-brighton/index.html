These are simple estimates, rounded and based on typical product use in a home your size. They are not a certified carbon footprint.

How we work these out
  • Bottles: a one-bedroom home gets through about half a single-use plastic bottle of cleaning product per clean. We scale that by home size (1.35 for two bedrooms, up to 2.5 for five or more). We refill glass bottles from returnable 10-litre drums, so each one counts as saved.
  • Harsh chemicals: about 0.18 litres per clean, per size step, of products that carry a corrosive, irritant or environmental hazard label.
  • CO₂e: 0.1 kg per plastic bottle, 0.6 kg per litre of conventional product, and 0.8 kg per visit because our team travels by e-cargo bike and electric van rather than a petrol van.
  • Miles: an average petrol car at roughly 0.27 kg CO₂e per mile, rounded to the nearest 5 miles.

Often contains: formic or sulfamic acid, non-ionic surfactants, perfume (parfum), limonene, colourant.

What we use instead

Citric acid spray and castile soap

A 5% citric acid solution dissolves limescale on taps, screens and shower heads. Soap scum comes off with diluted castile soap and a microfibre cloth, then a squeegee so it builds up more slowly.

Honest result: As good on limescale. Thick soap scum can need a second pass on the first visit.

We dilute it to 5% on site. The concentrate is an eye irritant, so it stays in our kit and leaves with us.

Typical of the product type, not of any particular brand. Individual products vary, so always read the label on your own bottles, and never mix cleaning products.

Do eco products really clean as well as bleach?

For everyday cleaning, yes. Dirt, grease and limescale come off with the right acid, alkali or soap and a good cloth, and that's exactly what we use. Bleach is mainly a whitener and disinfectant, so for whitening we use an oxygen-based whitener, and where something really needs disinfecting we use steam or alcohol on just those spots. Where eco methods are slower, like years of baked-on oven carbon, we tell you and allow more time.

How do I know this isn't greenwashing?

Ask us anything. Every product has a printed ingredient list in your cleaner's kit and at the refill station, and we'll email it to you before your first clean. Our impact figures are simple estimates and we show the working. We'll also tell you when eco isn't the answer, like mould that needs new sealant rather than more scrubbing.

Is it safe around babies, pets and allergies?

That's why we started. No synthetic fragrance, no aerosols, and floors get a clean-water rinse so little hands and paws don't pick anything up. We skip essential oils in homes with cats, birds, rabbits or under-twos, and our vacuums have HEPA filters. Tell us about allergies on the booking form and we'll note them on your cleaner's sheet.

Do I need to be home, and how do you look after keys?

Most of our customers are out. Keys are given a code, never labelled with your address, and kept in a locked key safe at our North Laine office. Key safes and smart locks are fine too. Only your regular cleaner and our office manager can see your code.

Can you do same-day changeovers between guests?

Yes. We usually work between 11am and 3pm, or to your own check-out and check-in times. We clean, change the beds, restock your welcome basics and send a photo report to your phone. Hosts get a shared calendar link so new bookings land with us automatically.

What if something's missed?

Tell us within 48 hours and we'll come back and re-clean that area free. For changeovers, tell us before your guest arrives and we'll send someone straight back, or refund that clean if we can't get there in time.

Do you bring everything, including the vacuum?

Yes: refillable products, freshly washed microfibre cloths, mops, a HEPA vacuum and, for deep cleans, a steam cleaner. If you'd rather we used your own vacuum, that's fine too.

Are you insured, and how are cleaners vetted?

We carry £5 million public liability insurance. Every cleaner is employed by us (not self-employed), DBS-checked, referenced and trained in-house for two weeks before they clean on their own.
